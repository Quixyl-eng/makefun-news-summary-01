Wednesday, 11 November 2026. In Abu Dhabi, Russian and Ukrainian delegations held a second day of US-brokered talks, the first trilateral meeting since February. President Volodymyr Zelensky called the first day constructive and Secretary of State Marco Rubio called it very productive; envoy Jared Kushner said Washington had narrowed the gaps (BBC, AP). Zelensky later said Russia had claimed at the talks to occupy half of the Donbas, which he rejected, and that US mediators had confirmed Ukraine's battlefield assessment (Guardian).

In Antalya, a two-day COP31 leaders' summit opened, with Turkey proposing that electricity reach 35 percent of global energy use by 2035. President Recep Tayyip Erdogan and UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres were due to open the session. Speakers due included Brazil's Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva, EU Commission President Ursula von der Leyen, China's Han Zheng and Britain's Andy Burnham; Donald Trump and other G7 leaders are absent (AFP, Anadolu Agency).

The US House on Tuesday voted 211-204 to send Thomas Massie's bid to impeach Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, blocking it; Massie was the only Republican opposed (BBC, Anadolu Agency). In Mississippi, Democrat Scott Colom led Republican Sen. Cindy Hyde-Smith by about 2,500 votes, with certification due Friday (BBC). Washington on Tuesday charged three alleged Iran-backed operatives over plots including a September incident near RAF Fairford; Iran denies involvement (BBC).

Reuters and Al Jazeera reported Wednesday that Congo government data, released Tuesday, put Ebola cases at 11,042 and deaths at 5,387 in a Bundibugyo-strain outbreak in eight of 26 provinces. In Seongnam, south of Seoul, a city bus struck 32 vehicles at 7:34 a.m., injuring 57 people, nine seriously, Yonhap reported; the driver said the brakes failed. Asian markets were mixed after US October CPI rose 0.4 percent, above a 0.3 percent forecast, and the S&P 500 fell 0.38 percent on Tuesday (NDTV Profit, BLS).
