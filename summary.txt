Wednesday 11 November 2026. In Abu Dhabi, the second day of US-brokered talks between Ukraine and Russia ended with agreement on a large prisoner exchange, with the numbers to be set on Thursday. US envoy Jared Kushner called it the biggest humanitarian release under discussion, and Russian delegate Vladimir Medinsky confirmed the plan. President Volodymyr Zelensky, who did not attend, had called the opening day constructive, and Secretary of State Marco Rubio called that session very productive. In Gaza, local health officials said Israeli strikes and gunfire killed at least seven people, including a father and his 13-year-old son on a displacement shelter in Zeitoun, Gaza City, another person near a school in Daraj, and a man in Khan Younis. The Israeli military said it struck militants in Zeitoun and Daraj and did not comment on the later deaths. NATO scrambled jets after at least four drones entered Baltic airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia. Lithuania, Latvia and Estonia briefly warned residents to take shelter. Lithuanian Prime Minister Mindaugas Sinkevicius said it was a difficult night and that nobody was hurt. At COP31 in Turkey, UN Secretary-General Antonio Guterres told leaders that the climate response was being outrun and warned of a path to mutually assured destruction. Australia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu endorsed a declaration to double the number of homes with rooftop solar by 2030. Elsewhere on Wednesday, the US Justice Department charged three alleged Iran-backed operatives over plots in the United States and Europe, including an alleged plot at RAF Fairford, which Tehran denied. Italy evacuated at least 300 schools after identical bomb-threat messages that Education Minister Giuseppe Valditara called presumed hoaxes. Russia's FSB said it arrested two people it described as Polish agents gathering information in Kaliningrad. A UK final court of appeal struck down Trinidad and Tobago's colonial-era law banning gay sex.
